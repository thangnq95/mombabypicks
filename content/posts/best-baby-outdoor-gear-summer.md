## FAQ

### When can I take my newborn outside in summer?

Newborns under 6 months should avoid direct sun exposure entirely — their skin burns faster and they can't regulate body temperature reliably. Keep outings to early morning or late evening when temperatures are below 80°F, always use shade, and dress baby in lightweight, UPF-rated clothing. Check with your pediatrician about when to introduce sunscreen for your baby specifically.

### What temperature is too hot for a baby to be outside?

Most pediatricians recommend limiting outdoor time when the heat index exceeds 90°F. If you need to go out, keep it brief, stay in shade, offer extra feeds to prevent dehydration, and watch for signs of overheating: flushed skin, rapid breathing, or unusual fussiness. A shaded playard or stroller with UV canopy extends safe outdoor time even on warm days.

### Do stroller sun shades actually block UV?

It depends on the rating. Look for UPF 50+ — that blocks 98% of UV radiation. Standard stroller canopies without a UPF rating can let through significant UV light even when baby appears to be in shade. Mesh canopies without UPF ratings often provide minimal protection. The UPPAbaby MINU V2's extendable canopy is rated UPF 50+ across its full coverage area.

### Are reusable swim diapers as effective as disposable?

Yes — no swim diaper (reusable or disposable) is designed to absorb urine, because it would swell and fail in water. Both types are designed to contain solids only. The advantage of reusables is fit: well-made reusables like the LittleMissMatched have a snugger leg seal than many disposables, which reduces the chance of leaks in the water.

### How do I protect my baby's car seat from summer heat and moisture?

A waterproof liner like the Diono Ultra Dry Seat handles sweat and wet swimsuits. For hot buckles after parking, use a small towel or car seat cover over metal parts. Always check harness and buckle temperature before buckling baby in — metal components can reach dangerous temperatures in a parked car, even on a mildly warm day.
