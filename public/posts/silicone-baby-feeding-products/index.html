Introduction#

Silicone has largely replaced plastic in premium baby feeding products — and for good reason. Food-grade silicone is stable at high and low temperatures, free of BPA and phthalates, doesn’t absorb odors or stains, and is dishwasher-safe. It’s the closest thing to a safe, durable, and practical feeding material available for babies.

But not all silicone is equal. “Silicone” on a label doesn’t guarantee food-grade quality, and some cheaper products blend silicone with plastic fillers that compromise those properties. This guide covers what to look for, which products are worth buying, and which silicone items to prioritize for your baby.

For more on safe feeding materials, see our eco-friendly baby gear guide and the best baby bottles for newborns.

What Is Food-Grade Silicone?#

Food-grade silicone (also called platinum-cured silicone) is made by curing liquid silicone with a platinum catalyst. It produces a stable, inert polymer that doesn’t react with food, doesn’t leach chemicals, and is safe for repeated heating and cooling.

The simple test: pinch and twist a silicone product. If it turns white at the twist point, it likely contains plastic fillers. Pure food-grade silicone doesn’t change color under this stress.

Food-grade silicone is:

  • BPA-free, BPS-free, phthalate-free
  • Stable from -40°F to 446°F (-40°C to 230°C)
  • Dishwasher and microwave safe
  • Odor and stain resistant
  • Non-porous (doesn’t absorb bacteria)

How to Spot Inferior Silicone Products#

Watch for these red flags:

The pinch-and-twist test fails: Twist the silicone firmly. Whitening at the stress point = plastic fillers present.

Vague “silicone” labeling: Legitimate food-grade silicone products typically say “food-grade silicone,” “platinum-cured silicone,” or specifically state BPA/BPS/phthalate-free. “Silicone” alone isn’t enough.

Very low price for silicone products: True food-grade silicone costs more to produce than standard plastic. Extremely cheap “silicone” bibs or products are often blended with cheaper materials.

Strong smell: Food-grade silicone has very little odor. A strong plastic or chemical smell from a product labeled silicone suggests fillers.

Frequently Asked Questions#

Is silicone better than glass for baby bottles? Both are good choices. Glass is heavier and breakable; silicone is lighter and flexible. Glass is easier to verify as truly inert. Silicone is generally safer to drop. For most parents, food-grade silicone bottles offer the best balance of safety and practicality.

Can silicone baby products go in the dishwasher? Yes. Food-grade silicone is dishwasher-safe and doesn’t degrade with repeated dishwasher cycles the way some plastics do. It’s one of silicone’s strongest practical advantages.

How long do silicone baby products last? Food-grade silicone is highly durable. Bottles and spoons typically last as long as you need them. Check for any tearing, biting damage (especially on teethers), or color changes that might indicate degradation, and replace if you see these signs.

At what age can babies use silicone products? Depends on the product. Silicone bottles can be used from birth. Silicone teethers from about 3 months. Silicone bibs and plate systems from about 4–6 months when solids begin. Silicone straw cups from around 6 months.
